# Wikipedia interest signals

Use the bundled TypeScript CLI for repeatable collection, analysis, charting, and a one-page PDF. `tsx` runs the `.ts` source directly, with no build output. The numbers measure article requests, not people, product demand, or willingness to pay.

For an ordinary request, read only this file and the chosen manifest, run the CLI, then read `metrics.json`. The file paths are given here. **Do not inventory this directory with `rg --files`, `find .`, or recursive `ls`**: `npm ci` puts thousands of dependency files in `node_modules`, wasting a low-cost model's context. Open source files only to debug a failure.

## Run

From this skill directory:

```sh
npm ci
export WIKIMEDIA_USER_AGENT='WikipediaInterest/1.0 (your-real-contact@example.com)'
node --import tsx scripts/wiki-interest.ts discover --source en:Astronomy --languages uk,pl,cs
node --import tsx scripts/wiki-interest.ts search --language pl --query "astronomia"
node --import tsx scripts/wiki-interest.ts analyze --manifest examples/astronomy.json --out /tmp/astronomy-interest
```

Replace the example address with a **real operator contact** before making live requests. Wikimedia requires a descriptive User-Agent with an email, URL, or wiki user page in parentheses; `--user-agent` also works. Cached requests need no new contact. By default, the latest period ends at the prior UTC month, or two months back on UTC days 1–2 to allow publication lag. `--end YYYY-MM` sets an earlier end. Use `--cache DIR` across related runs and `--refresh` to bypass cached API responses when checking recent or previously missing data. The three outputs are `metrics.json` (exact values, flags, URLs, timestamps), `chart.svg` (indexed trend), and `report.pdf` (shareable summary). Run `node --import tsx scripts/wiki-interest.ts --help` for syntax. Developers can run `npm run typecheck` and `npm test`.

A point-in-time [Ukrainian astronomy example](examples/astronomy-output/manifest.json) includes its manifest, metrics, chart, and PDF for inspection.

## Choose pages before comparing

1. Identify a specific topic proxy. For an existing source article, use `discover` to get interlanguage titles. For an edition without a link, use `search` with a localized phrase and inspect candidates. A missing article is **not** evidence of no audience interest.
2. Check that each chosen page covers comparable concepts. Before writing a manifest, open [the example](examples/astronomy.json) and copy its `editions` shape; do not infer the schema. The minimum is `{"topic":"Astronomy","editions":[{"language":"uk","articles":["Астрономія"],"rationale":"Broad overview"}]}`. Put exact titles in `articles`, why they represent the user's topic in `rationale`, and important caveats in `assumptions`. Multiple articles are allowed but their views are summed, so the result does not count distinct readers. If the chosen pages deliberately cover different concepts, explain why in top-level `conceptMismatchRationale`; the CLI otherwise stops on different known Wikidata IDs before loading traffic. A matching ID supports page selection but does not prove that the article scope fits the question; a missing ID leaves the check unverified.
3. Add known older titles when relevant. Under an edition, use `"formerTitles":{"X (social network)":["Twitter"]}` when `"X (social network)"` is in its `articles` list. A selected title that actually redirects to the current page also supplies a hint. `analyze` checks the page move log against its stable page ID, uses one title for each full month, and makes a month containing a move unknown. A failed move check makes the affected article's history unknown. Without a known former title, history is labeled `unchecked`; this is not an exhaustive search for every past title.
4. Run `analyze`. It resolves redirects to current canonical titles, checks concept IDs and known title moves, fetches monthly article and edition traffic, and compares the most recent complete 12 months with the preceding 12. The default period is 24 complete months. For a follow-up, change the manifest or `--end`, reuse `--cache`, and save to a new output directory.

## Interpret

- `viewsYoyPct` compares matched 12-month windows, reducing simple seasonal bias. `shareRecentPerMillion` is selected article views per million views across that language edition; `shareYoyPct` shows whether the topic gained share as the edition changed size. Compare both raw and relative movement. If an edition month or any selected article's traffic is unavailable, or no article observations exist, the complete-window totals and growth fields are `null` rather than a distorted comparison; `observedViewsPrior/Recent` preserve counts that were actually observed.
- In each `results` entry, `articleMonthlyViews` shows each resolved article's counts, `editionMonthlyViews` shows the whole language edition's counts, and `monthlyViews` remains the combined article count. For example, if two articles have 120 and 80 views in `2026-08`, the combined value is 200; with 2,000,000 edition views that month, the topic has 100 selected-article views per million edition views. Annual share uses the sums across each 12-month window. `null` means unknown, while `0` means a verified zero. A combined month is `null` if any selected article or the edition month is unknown. Inspect individual articles when their trends disagree; the combined total can hide opposing movement. The PDF names a top recent article only when every selected article has 12 known recent months, and points to `metrics.json` for details.
- Inspect `peakPairSensitivity` beside the ordinary 12-month result. It picks the busiest combined article month, removes it **and the same calendar month a year apart**, then recalculates article growth and article share using the remaining 11 months of article and edition traffic in each year. For example, 100 views in every month except 1,100 in June 2026 produces +83.3% ordinary growth but 0% after removing June 2025 and June 2026. `excludedMonths` names the pair; `rawDirectionChanged` and `shareDirectionChanged` flag a change between positive, flat, and negative growth. `incomplete-data` means an article or edition month is unknown, including a move month; `zero-traffic` means no meaningful peak; `zero-baseline` keeps 11-month counts but leaves growth percentages `null`. This check does not alter `selectedCandidate`, so explicitly warn when a selected lead depends on one month. The PDF's `11m raw/share` column is compact; `*` marks changed direction, and `metrics.json` has exact values.
- `articleTrends` gives each selected article's prior/recent 12-month views, percentage change, and `rising`, `falling`, `flat`, or `unavailable` direction. Explain opposing article trends instead of relying on their sum. An article rising from zero has a valid direction but `viewsYoyPct: null`; an article with an unknown month has no complete trend. One complete article may still have a trend when another is unavailable. The PDF names individual article directions when they fit and otherwise gives counts; `metrics.json` contains every article.
- `positiveYoyMonths` and `negativeYoyMonths` compare each month with the same month a year earlier. `direction` is `rising` after at least 8 increases, `falling` after at least 8 decreases, or `mixed` otherwise. `peakMonthShare` checks whether a single month dominates either year. The existing JSON key `confidence` means **signal quality** in the PDF and response: it describes coverage, traffic volume, monthly consistency, and spikes, not statistical confidence or customer demand. High requires 24 loaded edition months, at least 1,000 views in each year, 9 of 12 comparisons in one direction, and no month above 25% of its year's views. Medium needs 200 views in each year, 7 directional comparisons, and no month above 35%. A steady decline can have high signal quality; missing data makes it insufficient.
- The default screen requires at least 200 recent selected-article views, medium or high signal quality, positive raw article-view growth, positive growth in share of the whole edition, and `direction: rising`. The manifest's `screening` object can adjust the existing thresholds and growth requirements; `requireRisingDirection: false` explicitly disables the monthly-direction check. `results[].screeningAssessment` gives each edition's five ordered checks, observed values, requirements, `pass`/`fail`/`not-required` statuses, explanations, and `eligibleRank`. Report every edition's failed checks. Rank 1 is `selectedCandidate`; another edition can pass but rank lower. High signal quality alone does not make a decline eligible. `nextValidationStep` gives a concrete user-research action. The selected edition is a research lead, not a launch recommendation.
- Check `conceptCheck`, `flags`, article fit, redirects, API status, source URLs, and retrieval times in `metrics.json`. Different known Wikidata IDs require a recorded review reason; missing IDs are `unverified`. New analyses give each source article a `monthStatus`: `reported` means Wikimedia supplied a count, `zero` means a successful article response omitted that month while edition traffic exists, and `unknown` means the article response was unavailable, a move month cannot be assigned, or the omission could not be checked against edition traffic. For a known former title, inspect `historyStatus`, `moveEvents`, `moveLookupUrls`, `uncertainMonths`, and `trafficSegments`; each segment names the title and inclusive months used for its traffic request. `articleMonthlyViews` stays keyed by the current canonical title. The saved point-in-time example predates these audit fields, the monthly breakdown, `peakPairSensitivity`, and `articleTrends`. A 404, exhausted transient request, or uncertain move history blocks complete growth and candidate selection for its edition; an authorization or malformed-response error still stops the run. `analyticsError` records a transient request failure for a single segment, and multi-segment errors appear in `trafficSegments`. Editions cover readers across countries.
- The PDF bundles fonts for Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, and CJK text. If another glyph is unsupported, the CLI gives an explicit error. The indexed chart requires a complete positive prior-year baseline; a missing line or the unavailable notice means the series cannot be plotted as a valid index.

In the response, state the period, exact proxy articles, ordinary raw and relative changes, the 11-month check and excluded pair when available, individual article disagreements, signal quality, why each edition passed or failed the screen, an actionable validation step, and limits. If signals disagree or are weak, say so. Ask for a different proxy or timeframe when that would change the answer. For larger research programs, use the staged extensions in [development roadmap](references/roadmap.md).
